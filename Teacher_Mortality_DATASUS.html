Mortality among teachers in Brazil

A descriptive analysis of SIM/DATASUS, 2010–2024

Felipe Barletta

06 September 2026

1 DATASUS and health data in Brazil

Why DATASUS matters. Brazil’s Department of Information and Informatics of the Unified Health System (DATASUS) is part of the Ministry of Health and supports the development, maintenance, operation, integration and dissemination of national health information systems. These systems are central to health surveillance, planning, management and research.

DATASUS was created in 1991 and today supports a broad ecosystem of national health-information systems and digital platforms. For researchers, its importance lies in the availability of large, routinely collected administrative and epidemiological datasets that cover different stages of healthcare and vital events.

Examples of information available through the Brazilian Ministry of Health and DATASUS include:

Table 1.1: Selected Brazilian health information systems
System Main information Example use
SIM Deaths and underlying causes of death Mortality and cause-of-death analyses
SINASC Live births and maternal/newborn characteristics Maternal and child health research
SIH/SUS Hospital admissions financed by the SUS Hospital utilization and outcomes
SIA/SUS Outpatient procedures financed by the SUS Outpatient care and procedure monitoring
CNES Health establishments, services and workforce Health-service capacity and infrastructure

This project focuses on the Sistema de Informações sobre Mortalidade (SIM). SIM was developed by the Ministry of Health in 1975 and is based on information recorded on the Declaração de Óbito (Death Certificate). The system contains variables that allow mortality patterns and underlying causes of death to be studied across Brazil.

Official information:

1.1 From DATASUS to the research question

SIM contains information on the date of death, age, sex, race/skin color, municipality of residence, occupation and underlying cause of death, among other fields. The combination of occupation (CBO) and underlying cause of death (ICD-10) makes it possible to describe mortality among people whose occupation was recorded as a teaching occupation.

Project objective. To describe deaths among teachers recorded in the Brazilian Mortality Information System between 2010 and 2024, characterize their demographic and cause-of-death profiles, and examine the temporal pattern of suicide and homicide.

This is a descriptive mortality analysis. The results are counts and proportions among deaths recorded in SIM; they are not mortality rates, because the analysis does not use the number of teachers in the Brazilian population as a denominator.

2 Data and cohort definition

2.1 Study period and variables

The study covers 2010–2024. Only variables required for the analysis are downloaded to reduce memory use.

Table 2.1: SIM variables used in this project
Variable Description
DTOBITO Date of death
OCUP Occupation according to the Brazilian Classification of Occupations (CBO)
CAUSABAS Underlying cause of death according to ICD-10
SEXO Sex recorded on the death certificate
IDADE Age at death
RACACOR Race/skin color
CODMUNRES Municipality of residence

2.2 Who is considered a teacher?

The operational definition identifies records whose processed CBO occupation description contains the Portuguese root “professor”. The original CBO code is retained for auditing.

Population represented in this project. The resulting dataset includes teaching occupations across different educational levels, from early-childhood and basic education to vocational education, free courses and higher education, including university professors. The SIM occupation field used here does not distinguish public-sector from private-sector employment. Therefore, the analysis should not be interpreted as referring only to public-school teachers.

Occupation in SIM is the occupation reported on the death certificate. It should not be interpreted as a complete employment history or proof that the person was actively employed in that occupation at the time of death.

3.3 Download SIM and identify teachers

To avoid holding the complete national SIM database for all 15 years in memory, each year is downloaded, processed and filtered separately. Only teacher records are retained before the next year is processed.

sim_variables <-
  c(
    "DTOBITO",
    "OCUP",
    "CAUSABAS",
    "SEXO",
    "IDADE",
    "RACACOR",
    "CODMUNRES"
  )

download_teacher_deaths <-
  function(year) {

    message(
      "Downloading and processing SIM data for ",
      year,
      "..."
    )

    sim_raw <-
      fetch_datasus(
        year_start = year,
        year_end = year,
        uf = "all",
        information_system = "SIM-DO",
        vars = sim_variables,
        stop_on_error = TRUE,
        timeout = 600,
        quiet = FALSE
      )

    # Preserve the original CBO code before process_sim() converts
    # occupation codes to occupation descriptions.
    sim_raw <-
      sim_raw %>%
      mutate(
        OCUP_CODE =
          as.character(
            OCUP
          )
      )

    sim_processed <-
      process_sim(
        sim_raw,
        municipality_data = FALSE
      )

    teachers_year <-
      sim_processed %>%
      filter(
        !is.na(
          OCUP
        )
      ) %>%
      filter(
        str_detect(
          str_to_lower(
            as.character(
              OCUP
            )
          ),
          "professor"
        )
      ) %>%
      mutate(
        YEAR = year
      )

    rm(
      sim_raw,
      sim_processed
    )

    gc()

    teachers_year
  }

teacher_rds <-
  file.path(
    data_directory,
    "teacher_deaths_SIM_2010_2024.rds"
  )

if (file.exists(teacher_rds)) {

  teacher_deaths <-
    readRDS(
      teacher_rds
    )

} else {

  teacher_deaths <-
    purrr::map_dfr(
      years,
      download_teacher_deaths
    )

  saveRDS(
    teacher_deaths,
    teacher_rds,
    compress = TRUE
  )
}

8 External causes and intentional violence

8.1 Classification

The analysis next focuses on ICD-10 Chapter XX, which contains external causes of morbidity and mortality. The categories used here are mutually exclusive.

teacher_deaths <-
  teacher_deaths %>%
  mutate(
    external_cause_category =
      case_when(
        str_detect(
          icd10_3,
          "^V"

Important distinction. Suicide is defined as ICD-10 X60–X84 and homicide/assault as X85–Y09. Events of undetermined intent (Y10–Y34) remain separate. Female homicide victims can be identified in SIM, but femicide cannot be established from the underlying ICD-10 cause alone because the legal and contextual information required to classify a homicide as femicide is not contained in this field.

10 Interpretation

Main descriptive finding. Across 2010–2024, the selected SIM records contain 3,278 intentional violent deaths among people whose occupation was recorded as a teaching occupation. Of these, 1,708 (52.1%) were classified as suicide and 1,570 (47.9%) as homicide.

The annual curves tell an additional story. Homicide represented the larger share of intentional violent deaths during most of the first part of the series. From 2018 onward, suicide accounted for the larger annual share, and the gap widened in the later years of the study period. In the final year, suicide represented approximately two-thirds of the suicide-plus-homicide deaths.

These findings are descriptive and should not be interpreted as evidence that teaching causes suicide or homicide, nor as evidence that teachers have higher risks than other occupational groups. Establishing relative risk would require an appropriate population denominator and a comparison population.

11 Limitations

Several limitations are important when interpreting the results:

  1. No population denominator. The analysis reports counts and proportions, not mortality rates.
  2. Occupation measurement. Occupation is the occupation recorded on the death certificate and may not represent current employment at death.
  3. Public versus private employment. The variables used here do not identify whether the teacher worked in the public or private sector.
  4. Broad teacher definition. The operational filter includes a wide range of teaching occupations, from basic to higher education and other teaching activities.
  5. Changes in occupation recording. Temporal patterns may be influenced by changes in completeness or coding of the occupation field.
  6. Intent and legal classification. ICD-10 identifies assault/homicide and intentional self-harm, but it does not by itself identify femicide.
  7. Descriptive design. The observed temporal trends do not establish causal mechanisms.
